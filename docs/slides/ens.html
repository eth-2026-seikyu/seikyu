Seikyu on ENSv2

A marketplace for unpaid invoices, where the invoice is the name.

A business gets paid today; an investor buys the debt at a discount. Every invoice is an ENS name whose expiry is its due date.

Sepolia · sepolia-deployment-2026-09-15 · seikyu.eth

The problem

The work is done.
The money is 60 days away.

  • A supplier delivers today and is paid in 60 to 90 days
  • Nobody else will buy that debt, because nobody outside the deal can check it
  • The due date is a column in someone's database — it expires nothing, and it gates nothing

The solution

The invoice is the name, and the due date is the expiry

  • Eight text records anyone can read without an account · the debtor's accountant confirms the debt on-chain · the name stops resolving the day the debt falls due

How it works

Where the name lives and dies

Built on ENSv2

Expiry is an economic primitive, not a label

  • Pinned to sepolia-deployment-2026-09-15; main's API differs from what's deployed
  • Own UserRegistry via VerifiableFactory, under seikyu.eth
  • inv-15.seikyu.eth's expiry equals the invoice's due date, exactly

A constraint we hit

One resolver per invoice — by force, not choice

  • Setter roles are scoped by keccak256(key), never by name
  • Debtor write-access to ack alone needs its own resolver
  • Eight text records per invoice, resolver address included

Enforced on-chain, shown live

Confirm the debt, touch nothing else

  • Debtor's AP wallet holds an EAC role scoped to ack alone
  • Editing the amount reverts: EACUnauthorizedAccountRoles
  • Confirming ack flips the badge to "Confirmed by debtor"

ENS state gates real money

A disputed invoice cannot be bought

  • InvoiceMarket.buy() checks REGISTRAR.isLive(id) before moving money
  • Refuses outright when the debtor's ack reads disputed
  • ENS state gates the money — not application logic

Two independent read paths

The name retires; the record doesn't

  • closeInvoice pays the investor, burns the token, unregisters — one tx
  • Liveness reads the registry; records read the resolver cached at registration
  • Registry returns address(0) once expired — the cache keeps records alive

What we'd tell the ENS team

The deploy tag should be
the docs entry point.

Key-scoped setter roles deserve a prominent note — it drove our whole resolver-per-invoice design. The missing text() getter forces resolve + multicall. And renew reviving an expired name's storage is useful, though undocumented.
