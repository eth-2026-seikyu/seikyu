Seikyu 請求

Get paid today for an invoice that is due in 60 days.

A marketplace for unpaid invoices — each one becomes an ENS name anyone can check, and it expires the day the debt is due.

Live on Sepolia · seikyu.xyz

The problem

The work is done.
The money is 60 days away.

  • A supplier delivers today and is paid in 60 to 90 days
  • Payroll, materials and rent do not wait that long
  • A bank will lend against the invoice — after collateral, paperwork and two weeks

Why nobody just buys it

The invoice is a PDF,
and a PDF proves nothing.

  • Was the work really delivered? Only the debtor company knows
  • Has the same invoice already been sold to someone else?
  • Checking costs more than the discount is worth — so the supplier waits

The solution

Make the invoice checkable, and it becomes sellable

  • Supplier lists an unpaid invoice · Investor buys it today at a discount · Debtor company confirms the debt, then pays in full

How it works

The whole deal, start to finish

Step 1 — Issue

One signature turns an invoice into an asset

  • Mints an ERC-721 receivable
  • Deploys a resolver for this invoice alone
  • Registers inv-15.seikyu.eth with expiry = the due date

Step 1 — Issue

The invoice is the ENS name

  • Eight text records: amount, debtor, due date, status, issuer…
  • Anyone can read it — no Seikyu account needed
  • The name lives exactly as long as the debt is current

Step 2 — Buy

Only a real person can buy

  • World ID proves the buyer is one unique human
  • The proof is bound to their wallet, server-side
  • One human, one investor wallet — capped at 3 open invoices

Step 2 — Buy

950 now,
instead of 1,000 later

  • The supplier is paid the moment the sale clears
  • The ENS status record flips to funded

Step 3 — The debtor confirms

They can confirm the debt — and change nothing else

  • The debtor's accountant writes one record: ack
  • Editing the amount reverts on-chain, by design
  • A disputed invoice cannot be bought at all

Step 4 — Settle

Paid in full, and the identity retires with the debt

  • The investor receives the full 1,000
  • The receivable token is burned
  • The ENS name is unregistered — same transaction

Why expiry matters

An unsold invoice
simply expires

  • No cleanup job — the due date is the expiry
  • The name stops resolving; the records stay readable
  • Unpaid but sold? Anyone can extend it 30 days
